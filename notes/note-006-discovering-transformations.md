# Note 006 — Discovering Transformations

*Evolving note*

How can investigated solutions produce an enlarged repertoire of structural transformations? Recording an existing operation, finding another place to use it, and inventing a new construction are different changes. This note considers how to tell them apart and how a proposed transformation might be made precise enough to investigate.

## 1. Four kinds of change

| Situation | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Recording an existing operator | The notebook's coverage |
| Finding another interpretation | The operator's known applications |
| Discovering a useful composition or abstraction | The repertoire's organization and practical power |
| Inventing a structural transformation | The available construction mechanisms |

These situations can overlap in one investigation. For example, a composition of familiar operations may become a reusable method worth recording as its own operator. Calling it a new method does not establish that it is historically new. The distinction here is about what changes in the working model, while originality requires a separate search.

## 2. Possible routes from solutions to operators

Several investigations could suggest a candidate transformation:

- **Compare solution traces.** Align the steps taken in different solved problems and ask which relations, data dependencies, or invariants recur.
- **Identify repeated steps.** If a subprocedure recurs with stable inputs, outputs, and guarantees, it may deserve its own operator entry.
- **Decompose a construction.** Separate a large transformation into smaller operations, then test whether the pieces have independent contracts or useful recombinations.
- **Change the representation.** A difficult rule may become explicit after adding state, changing coordinates, taking a quotient, or preserving a correspondence map.
- **Study failed transfers.** A counterexample can reveal a missing applicability condition, a discarded invariant, or two operator families that should not share one schema.

These are research routes, not guaranteed invention procedures. A proposed abstraction can be too broad to have a useful contract, too narrow to transfer, or a restatement of an existing method.

## 3. A composition can be a method

Suppose an investigation combines $O_1$ and $O_2$ into a compatible composite $O_2\circ O_1$. The composite might contribute in several ways:

- it reaches outputs unavailable from the previously explored sequences;
- it reduces computation or memory under a stated cost model;
- it works under weaker assumptions than a known construction;
- it supplies a preservation guarantee that neither step alone describes;
- it makes a useful intermediate result available to later operations.

Each claim needs its own comparison. Additional reachability can be checked against the prior repertoire; cost needs an implementation and cost model; weaker assumptions need a precise statement of both contracts; and preservation needs declared observations and a proof or test. A composition is a candidate method when it has a repeatable interface and a purpose worth evaluating.

The state-expansion examples in Note 002 suggest how this could proceed. Determinizing an automaton and augmenting a duration-dependent state with elapsed age are established constructions. Their comparison raises questions about what context is encoded and which behavior is preserved. A further expansion–pruning–reduction sequence would need its own compatible conditions; shared vocabulary alone does not make the steps safe.

## 4. What changes in a categorical model?

Note 004 considers a category of objects and constructions, together with semantic interpretations as functors. One way to model an operator repertoire is as a presentation: chosen objects, generating constructions, and equations between composites.

Adding a generating construction can add new arrows and composites to the abstract model. Adding an equation can identify composites that were previously distinct. Revising an equation can also change which interpretations count as valid. In category-theoretic terms, one may form a category generated by the chosen constructions and then quotient by the declared equations; the details depend on the selected objects and typing rules.

Suppose an existing interpretation is a functor $F_D:\mathcal A\to\mathcal D$. If the revised model adds a generator $g:X\to Y$, extending $F_D$ requires a domain construction $F_D(g):F_D(X)\to F_D(Y)$ with a suitable contract. If the revised model imposes $u=v$, the interpreted composites must satisfy $F_D(u)=F_D(v)$. Thus an interpretation of the old model may fail to extend, or may need additional evidence, when the repertoire changes.

This gives a concrete question for invention: which existing semantic interpretations can realize the candidate generator, and which proposed equations do they preserve? A categorical model can make those obligations explicit. It does not supply the generator or prove that the new construction is useful.

## 5. Record a candidate with an explicit contract

A proposed operator should be a checkable entry, not just a name. A provisional record could include:

$$
O_{\mathrm{new}}=(T_{\mathrm{in}},T_{\mathrm{out}},\Theta,\Gamma,p,Q),
$$

with its representation language, admissible domain interpretations, input and output correspondence maps, resource assumptions, evidence, and known counterexamples. The contract $\Gamma\Rightarrow Q$ says when the construction is intended to apply and what it should preserve. The obligation remains open until justified.

Its investigation can then ask whether the construction is valid under that contract, useful for a stated goal, cheaper or more general than an alternative, compatible with the existing repertoire, and previously known in the stated literature or application scope. Those are separate questions with different evidence.

## 6. Questions to carry forward

Can trace comparison find stable transformations without discarding domain conditions? Which failed transfers lead to a useful refinement rather than an exception list? When does a composition deserve its own operator entry? How should a repertoire record historical provenance separately from formal reachability?

The intended outcome of this note is a **candidate operator with an explicit contract**, whose usefulness and originality remain questions to investigate.
